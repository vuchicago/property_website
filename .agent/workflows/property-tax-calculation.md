description: HOW TO CALCULATE YOUR PROPERTY TAX BILL IN COOK COUNTY

#The past four or five posts have demonstrated that most people are unaware as to how our Property Tax Bills are actually calculated here in Cook County, so this morning's Sunday Session with The Education Esquire will be a single POST which explains in detail how our property tax bill(s) are calculated and why they are so expensive in the ##Southern Suburbs of Chicago. 
1) FIRST STEP, REASSESSMENT: Every 3 years, the Assessor places new values on our properties called Assessed Property Values. This number is in the right-hand top corner of our property tax portal information which can be found online at cookcountypropertyinfo.com and in the bottom lefthand corner of our actual tax bill. This number is ESSENTIAL to determining what our eventual property tax bill. THIS is what you challenge when you file an appeal and how you can seek SOME property tax relief. It is the Assessor's equivalency to "MARKET VALUE". 
2) SECOND STEP, 10% OF ASSESSED VALUE: This is when the Assessor uses only 10% of the assessed value for residential real property (including vacant lots) here in Cook County because we have so many lots and parcels to assess. Other counties use 33 1/3 Assessment Values, but Cook County is unique and the only county in the state which relies on a 10% Assessed Value calculation system in the State of Illinois. 
3) THIRD STEP, MULTIPLY BY THE STATE EQUALIZER: Because Cook County is on a 10% assessed value system, the Illinois Department of Revenue (ILDOR) has to reset us back to as close to 33 1/3 as possible so we can be in alignment with the rest of the state of Illinois. How the ILDOR accomplishes that is by reviewing all homes sales in Cook County from the previous year and then issuing what is called a State Equalizer for Cook County which in 2024 was exactly 3.0355. This value helps for Cook County to be relatively close to the 33 1/3 Assessment value that the rest of Illinois uses, although it's rarely ever 33 1/3, as when one multiplies 10% x 3.0355 that only equals 30.355 which is 3% less than 33.333, but, it's an "ATTEMPT" not an exact science. 
4) FOURTH STEP, OBTAIN YOUR EQUALIZED ASSESSED VALUE: This step is purely mathematical. If your home's Estimated Property Value is $410,000 (like the house in the photo) then one would just do the basic math from there. $410,000 x .10 = $41,000 (Assessed Value) x 3.0355 (State Equalizer) = $124,455.56 (rounded up to $124,456) which equals this property's Equalized Assessed Value. 
5) FIFTH STEP, MULTIPLY BY THE LOCAL TAX RATE: This is the MOST CRITICAL STEP and what the TRUE PROBLEM IS IN THE STATE OF ILLINOIS. The Local Tax Rate fluctuates tremendously throughout the State of Illinois and can be as low as 6% in extremely wealthy communities and as high as 34% in extremely poor countries. In this case of the example, the Local Tax Rate is 17.601587% which is almost 3 times as high as what it is in Hinsdale, Illinois. This value is the PRIMARY COMPONENT IN HOW HIGH ONE'S PROPERTY TAX BILL IS and what the big fight is to go to a FLAT PROPERTY TAX RATE FOR THE ENTIRE STATE OF ILLINOIS. In continuing with the BASIC MATH, we would MULTIPLE $124,456 X .17601587 which equals $21,906.14 which would be this property's TAX LIABILITY BEFORE EXEMPTIONS. 
6) SIXTH STEP, SUBTRACT EXEMPTIONS: This is the step most people are really mystified by and that's because most people don't even understand how exemptions work. However, it's SUPER SIMPLE. For most people, they're only qualifying for the Homeowner's Exemption, and because we're on a 10% Assessment Valuation, it's super easy to know what the Homeowner's Exemption will be. It's WHATEVER YOUR TAX RATE IS TIMES 100!!!! SUPER EASY RIGHT? In this illustration, just take 17.601587 X 100 AND IT EQUALS $1,760.15 which is the VALUE OF THE HOMEOWNER'S EXEMPTION. We then SUBTRACT THAT DISCOUNT(EXEMPTION) FROM THE OVERALL TAX LIABILITY WHICH LOOKS LIKE THIS: $21,906.14 - $1,760.15 = $20,145.99 (which is what the actual tax bill should be, but unfortunately the Assessor doesn't properly round on the exemption breakdown, so this bill was A FEW PENNIES MORE)!!! 
THAT'S IT! SIX EASY STEPS TO CALCULATE AND COMPUTE YOUR PROPERTY TAX BILL, but most of us have NEVER BEEN TAUGHT HOW THAT PROCESS WORKS.

$600k x .10 = $60k (Assessed Value) x 3.0335 (2024 Equalizer) = $182,130 (Equalized Assessed Value) x .34396 (2024 Phoenix, Il Tax Rate) = $62,645.43 (Before exemption Property Tax Liability) - $3,439.60 (Homeowner's Exemption Value) = $59,205.83.
